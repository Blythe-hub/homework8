Fitting Exponential Growth Models For exponential growth models, use the formula:

\(\text{deaths} = \text{deaths}_0 \times e^{rt}\)

where \(r\) is the growth rate and \(t\) is time (days since first death).

Taking the log of both sides:

\(\log(\text{deaths}) = \log(\text{deaths}_0) + rt\)

Can fit a linear model to the log of deaths:

The growth rate represents the rate at which the number of daily deaths was increasing exponentially during this period. A higher growth rate means faster exponential growth. The doubling time is the time it takes for the number of daily deaths to double, calculated as ln(2)/growth rate. From our analysis, Spain appears to have had a slightly higher growth rate than Italy during this initial wave, resulting in a shorter doubling time for daily deaths. This suggests that in the early stages of the pandemic, the daily death count was increasing at a faster pace in Spain compared to Italy.
